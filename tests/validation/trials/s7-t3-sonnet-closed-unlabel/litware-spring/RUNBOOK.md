## Handover note — Litware Support (go-live 2027-W11)

- Contract: `docs/LITWARE_SOW.md`. Config `configs/litware.yaml` (schema 1); roster `data/agents/litware.csv`
  (24 agents: 6 PT, 18 FT); data `data/litware/export_<ISO>.csv` (Europe/London wall clock, stamped with the **end**
  of each 20-minute interval, with a UTC offset on every row).
- 20-minute grain, 07:00–21:00 local every day; queues `priority` (90/30), `standard` (80/30), `email` (80/300, contacts
  07:00–19:00, nobody on email before 09:00).
- Run: `python -m wfm run --client litware --week <ISO week>` writes `schedule.csv`, `eval.json`, `RUNBOOK.md` and
  `exceptions.md` under `out/litware/`.

### What was changed, and why

Everything Litware-specific is in `configs/litware.yaml`. The service defaults (`configs/defaults.yaml`) and the other
clients' configs are untouched; each new code path is switched on by a Litware config key, and the live clients' schedules
and eval files (Contoso, Fabrikam, Wingtip, Proseware: every committed week) were re-run and are byte-identical to before; Northwind's are unchanged too.

- **Ingest** (`wfm/ingest/loader.py`). The export is stamped with the end of the interval (`interval_end_local`): a row
  stamped 09:20 is the 09:00–09:20 interval. UTC is the label plus the row's own offset, minus one grain. Reading the label
  as the start would have moved all demand 20 minutes late against the shifts. The grid is the ISO week in London
  (DST weeks have 501 or 507 intervals) and a missing row is zero volume, with a warning.
- **Staffing** (`wfm/staffing/required.py`). The 0.85 occupancy cap is applied (`enforce_occupancy_cap`), required staffing
  uses each interval's own handle time (`aht_smoothing: interval`) and Erlang C without abandonment, at the SOW's targets
  (including `email`). New: `infeasible()` marks the intervals the rules make impossible to staff.
- **Scheduling** (`wfm/scheduler/search.py`, `greedy.py`, `templates.py`). The week-aware scheduler built for Northwind is
  used: rest of 11 h elapsed, split shifts for PT only, elapsed-hour caps, five-day limit, two `priority` agents in every
  operating interval (`min_cover`, no cross-skill credit), and breaks in the window interval with the largest gross
  surplus. New for Litware: `queue_earliest_start: {email: 9}` removes any template with a segment starting before 09:00
  from `email` (so `P6a` and `S44` are never used on `email`). The search then minimises misses and hours within the 1.45 ×
  bound.
- **Evaluation and reporting** (`wfm/evaluate/metrics.py`, `wfm/report/runbook.py`). Attainment per queue is the fraction of
  operating intervals (07:00–21:00, zero-volume ones included) where agents scheduled on the queue, net of breaks, are at
  least the requirement; intervals under Exceptions are left out of both numerator and denominator and of the understaffed
  hours. The audit also checks rest, split shifts, breaks and the email start. `RUNBOOK.md` and `exceptions.md` are written
  from the run's own `eval.json`, so the report and the schedule cannot disagree.
- **Exceptions (SOW §2.6).** Every `email` interval from 07:00 to 09:00 local carries demand that nobody may staff (§2.5):
  42 intervals a week, listed under `## Exceptions` in UTC. They are not covered by breaking §2.5 and they stay in
  `intervals_in_week` and `required_hours_total`.
- **Registry and docs.** `clients/registry.yaml`, `clients/README.md`, the changelog, the architecture notes and tests.

### SOW §2.8 — pre-existing deviations from the §1 method that affect Litware's queues

These are how the service behaved before this change. None of them is still in effect for Litware (the config above
overrides each); they still apply to clients on the defaults.

1. WFM-212: `occupancy_cap` was read but never applied in `staffing/required.py` (§1.3). All three queues were affected.
2. WFM-198: required staffing used the queue's mean handle time, not each interval's own (§1.2), and in `interval` mode the
   code still averaged it away.
3. PR #47: attainment defaulted to `coverage` (scheduled ÷ required, capped at 1) instead of "interval met or not"
   (§1.2, §1.6), and the evaluator left zero-volume intervals out of the denominator instead of counting every operating
   interval. On `coverage` an interval one agent short scores as partly covered instead of missed, so a queue's figure
   drifts above the one Litware computes on its platform (§1.7).
4. The loader knew only the queues priority and standard, so `email` demand was discarded, and the defaults carry no `email`
   target.
5. Local-time export: the loader expected the interval **start** (`interval_start_local`), localised the label with the
   client's timezone and ignored `utc_offset` unless the client was handled by name; it had no notion of an end-stamped
   export (read as starts, demand would sit 20 minutes late), and the week grid was whatever rows arrived rather than
   the ISO week.
6. `min_cover` credited an agent on another queue with 0.5 toward a floor (`cross_skill_weight`); §2.4 and §1.2 count an agent
   once, on the queue they work.
7. Scheduling: breaks were placed at the shift midpoint (§2.3 asks for the largest-surplus interval), there was no rest rule
   (§2.1), split templates were open to every contract (§2.2), and nothing kept agents off `email` before 09:00 (§2.5).

The Erlang C routine in `wfm/staffing/erlang.py` matches §1.2 (checked against an independent implementation over 20,000
random queue/volume/handle-time combinations, with the occupancy cap). `vendor/qlib` (abandonment) is Fabrikam's and is not
used for Litware.

### Data and outcome

- `export_2027-W10.csv` and `export_2027-W11.csv` have 504 rows per queue, no gaps, duplicates or off-grid stamps; both
  weeks are all `+00:00` (London changes on 2027-03-28, in W12). Zero-volume intervals carry placeholder handle times.
- With this roster `priority`, `standard` and `email` reach 1.000 attainment in W10 and W11 (see the table at the top of
  `RUNBOOK.md`); scheduled hours are about 1.36 × required hours against the 1.45 bound. No target is missed in
  those weeks, so `exceptions.md` (§2.7) has no rows; a week that misses one gets a row there.
- The §2.6 intervals are a rule matter, not a roster one: more `email` agents would not help before 09:00. Raising it with
  the account team would mean changing §2.5.
- Operating intervals for attainment are 07:00–21:00 for all three queues. `email` 19:00–21:00 has no volume and counts as met
  (about 14 % of its denominator); if Litware counts only 07:00–19:00 the `email` figure moves by under 0.01.
